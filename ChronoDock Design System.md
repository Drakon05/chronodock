# ChronoDock — Website Design System

**Version 1.0 · Strategy translated from the ChronoDock pitch deck**

> **Brand line:** Designed to Hold. Built to Showcase.
>
> **Core idea:** A considered place for the watch you wear every day.

---

## 1. The brand, distilled

ChronoDock is not a watch box or a generic desk accessory. It is a compact, single-watch dock that protects a timepiece while making it part of the room. The website should sell that change in relationship: from leaving a watch on a surface to giving it a deliberate place.

### What the deck establishes

- **Problem:** watches are left on unprotected surfaces; storage-first products can be bulky and visually overbearing. *(Deck pp. 2–3)*
- **Primary audience:** young professionals, especially people who use one to three watches and care about an organised desk or bedside setup. Enthusiasts, collectors and premium gift buyers are secondary audiences. *(pp. 4–5)*
- **Product promise:** compact footprint, secure hold and display-oriented design for everyday interaction. *(pp. 6, 10)*
- **Possible differentiators:** integrated lighting and magnification are presented in the prototype section. *(p. 7)*
- **Material direction:** matte polymer/resin for the entry variant; wood, metal accents and more refined finishes for premium variants. *(pp. 13, 15–16)*
- **Portfolio:** Standard, Premium and Grand Edition, positioned from daily utility to collector-focused statement piece. *(pp. 13, 15)*
- **Feedback noted in the deck:** preference for a compact form and wood finish; areas to improve include size compatibility, stability and durability. *(p. 8)*

### Strategic interpretation for the website

**Positioning:** *A design object for the watch you reach for every day.*

**Personality:** quiet confidence, tactile, precise, thoughtful, modern and warm.

**The experience should feel:** like discovering a well-made object in a design gallery—not browsing a gadget catalogue.

**The website must communicate, in this order:**
1. The dock looks at home on a considered desk or bedside table.
2. It gives a frequently worn watch a dedicated, easy-to-reach place.
3. Its form and details are intentional, not merely functional.
4. The three finishes/tiers let customers choose their level of expression.
5. Practical details—fit, materials, dimensions, care and delivery—are easy to find.

### What ChronoDock is not

- A crowded watch-storage system.
- A flashy “tech gadget” brand full of neon, gradients and feature badges.
- An imitation of a heritage watchmaker with excessive ornament, gold trim or fake prestige language.
- A brand that relies on unsupported performance claims.

## 2. Brand messaging

### Recommended message hierarchy

| Use | Copy direction |
|---|---|
| Brand line | **Designed to Hold. Built to Showcase.** *(from the deck)* |
| Homepage headline | **Give your everyday watch a place of its own.** |
| Supporting line | A compact dock that keeps your watch close, protected and on display. |
| Product philosophy | Useful by design. Considered in every detail. |
| Standard tier | The everyday essential. |
| Premium tier | A warmer finish, made to be noticed. |
| Grand Edition | A statement piece for a timepiece worth showing. |
| Primary CTA | Explore the collection |
| Secondary CTA | See how it works |

Use this language as a direction, not as a substitute for confirmed specifications. Avoid terms like *scratch-proof*, *universal fit*, *3× magnification*, *premium materials* or *built-in illumination* as guarantees until the production design and test data support them.

### Voice rules

- **Specific over grandiose:** describe the form, material, action or benefit.
- **Calm over persuasive:** short confident statements; no strings of exclamation marks.
- **Tactile over technical:** “walnut grain and a soft-touch base” is better than “next-generation premium construction,” when factually accurate.
- **Useful over vague:** answer what it holds, where it fits, how it works and how to care for it.
- **Never manufacture scarcity:** no “exclusive,” “limited” or “collector-grade” claims without a real basis.

### Example copy

**Use:** “A dedicated place for the watch you reach for every morning.”  
**Avoid:** “The ultimate revolutionary luxury watch-storage solution.”

**Use:** “A compact silhouette. A clear view of the dial. No bulky case to open.”  
**Avoid:** “Unmatched innovation and the most premium experience ever.”

---

## 3. Visual concept: Warm precision

Build the identity around the contrast already present in the product: a clean, sculptural silhouette against the warmth of wood, brass-toned watch details and softly lit interiors.

**Three visual principles**

1. **Object first:** large, high-quality product photography; copy never competes with the object.
2. **Warm restraint:** bone, ink, walnut and muted brass; avoid a wall of tan cards.
3. **Precision through spacing:** generous negative space, aligned baselines, fine rules, crisp type and close-up detail shots.

The pitch deck’s sand palette and condensed headings are useful origins, but its repeated rounded cards, heavy shadows and dense layouts should not carry over to the premium website. Replace them with editorial spacing, flat surfaces, subtle borders and deliberate image crops.

---

## 4. Colour system

Use warm neutrals as the foundation. Dark surfaces should feel like a gallery wall or a walnut cabinet; brass is a detail colour, not a blanket accent.

| Token | Hex | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `--cd-ivory` | `#F5F1E9` | Main light page background |
| `--cd-bone` | `#E8DDCC` | Secondary light section / warm canvas |
| `--cd-sand` | `#C9A477` | Brand-origin accent, small highlights only |
| `--cd-walnut` | `#553B2E` | Wood-inspired section, captions, rich surfaces |
| `--cd-ink` | `#191714` | Main text and dark sections |
| `--cd-charcoal` | `#26231F` | Dark panels and footer |
| `--cd-brass` | `#A98049` | Fine rules, small icons and selected details |
| `--cd-stone` | `#82776A` | Secondary text on light surfaces |
| `--cd-line` | `#D4C8B7` | Dividers, borders and quiet separators |
| `--cd-white` | `#FFFEFB` | Elevated light surface and product cut-outs |

### Colour usage ratio

- 60–70% ivory / white / bone
- 20–25% ink / charcoal / walnut
- 5–10% sand / brass combined

### Contrast rules

- Use ink on ivory and bone for normal text.
- Use ivory on ink, charcoal and walnut.
- Reserve brass for large text, icons or decorative strokes unless contrast is checked. Do not use muted brass for small body copy.
- Never rely on colour alone to communicate product tier, errors or selection.

## 5. Typography

Use two primary typefaces and one restrained technical accent. Load only the weights actually used.

### Primary display — Instrument Serif

**Role:** high-impact hero statements, editorial section headlines and short brand statements.  
**Weights:** Regular 400; Italic 400 for occasional emphasis only.  
**Character:** contemporary, crafted, quietly luxurious.  
**Do not use for:** paragraphs, buttons, specs or long product descriptions.

### Utility / interface — Manrope

**Role:** body copy, navigation, product names, buttons, forms, pricing and product descriptions.  
**Weights:** 400 Regular, 500 Medium, 600 Semibold, 700 Bold.  
**Character:** clean and contemporary; keeps the product experience useful and legible.

### Technical accent — IBM Plex Mono

**Role:** small labels such as `01 / STANDARD`, dimensions, material codes, SKU labels and image captions.  
**Weights:** 400 Regular, 500 Medium.  
**Rule:** use sparingly. It should suggest precision, not make the entire site feel like a control panel.

### Type scale

| Style | Desktop | Mobile | Typeface / weight |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Display / hero | 72–88 px / 0.98–1.05 | 44–54 px / 1.02 | Instrument Serif 400 |
| Display / section | 52–64 px / 1.05 | 36–42 px / 1.08 | Instrument Serif 400 |
| H2 | 36–44 px / 1.12 | 30–34 px / 1.15 | Manrope 500–600 |
| H3 | 23–28 px / 1.2 | 21–24 px / 1.25 | Manrope 600 |
| Body / lead | 18–20 px / 1.55 | 17–19 px / 1.55 | Manrope 400 |
| Body | 16 px / 1.6 | 16 px / 1.55 | Manrope 400 |
| Caption | 13–14 px / 1.45 | 13 px / 1.45 | Manrope 500 |
| Eyebrow / spec | 11–12 px / 1.4, tracked 0.08em | 11–12 px | IBM Plex Mono 400 |
| Button | 14–15 px / 1 | 14–15 px | Manrope 600 |

**Typesetting rules**
- Keep body copy to a readable measure of roughly 55–70 characters per line.
- Use sentence case for navigation and headings; avoid all-caps paragraphs.
- Use letter spacing only for short uppercase labels.
- Use the serif for emotion and the sans serif for action. Never use three display styles in the same viewport.
- Keep headings compact; do not force every line to be centered.


## 6. Layout and spacing

**Grid:** 12 columns on desktop, 8 on tablet, 4 on mobile.  
**Maximum content width:** 1,280 px; hero/product compositions may run wider.  
**Desktop side gutters:** 64–80 px.  
**Tablet side gutters:** 32 px.  
**Mobile side gutters:** 20–24 px.  
**Section spacing:** 112–144 px desktop; 72–88 px mobile.  
**Card padding:** 24–32 px.  
**Spacing base:** 4 px.

Recommended spacing tokens: `4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 80, 112, 144`.

### Shape language

- Product imagery: preserve the real silhouette; use clean crops and understated framing.
- Cards: square or gently rounded corners, 4–8 px. Avoid large bubble-like radii.
- Buttons: 2–4 px radius, not pill-shaped by default.
- Dividers: 1 px, low contrast.
- Shadows: almost invisible; use only when an object genuinely needs lift. Prefer borders and tonal contrast.

## 7. Photography and art direction

### The photography should communicate

- **Scale:** the dock in context on a desk or bedside table.
- **Craft:** wood grain, edge quality, finish transitions, lens and watch-dial details.
- **Use:** the simple moment of placing or picking up the watch.
- **Presence:** how the object looks when it is not being touched.

### Shot list for a launch-ready site

1. Hero: one dock, one watch, warm directional light, quiet background and generous negative space for the headline.
2. Three-quarter product view showing the full silhouette.
3. Front close-up showing the watch face and viewing area.
4. Material macro for each finish—only materials that exist in production.
5. Scale image on a realistic desk/bedside surface.
6. Hand placing or retrieving a watch, with natural proportions.
7. Back / base / contact points to demonstrate stability and construction.
8. Variant lineup: Standard, Premium and Grand in one consistent frame.
9. Packaging and gifting shot if the packaging is ready.
10. Dark, controlled hero image for Grand Edition.

### Lighting and colour grade

Soft window light or controlled warm key light; directional rather than flat. Keep whites warm-neutral, blacks detailed and wood natural. Metallic highlights should be subtle, not oversaturated. Use real prototypes whenever possible.

### Avoid

- Busy desks, artificial plants as filler, generic luxury watches unrelated to the product, excessive golden flares, fake lens glow, stock “businessman” photography, inconsistent render styles or AI-generated product geometry that changes the design.

## 8. Website structure

### Recommended top-level navigation

**Collection · How It Works · Our Approach · Journal**  
Utility actions: **Search (optional) · Cart**

Keep navigation short. On launch, one collection page can contain all variants; split into separate product pages when the buying experience and inventory warrant it.

### Homepage sequence

1. **Hero — The object.** Product image first. One clear headline, one supporting sentence, one primary action.
2. **Need — A better place for your watch.** Explain the everyday problem in a single concise paragraph.
3. **Design promise — Hold / Access / Display.** Three benefits supported by product imagery, not icon-heavy cards.
4. **Product in context.** Show its footprint and how it sits on a real desk or bedside table.
5. **Choose your finish.** Standard, Premium and Grand Edition in a considered comparison.
6. **Details that matter.** Fit, dimensions, materials, finish, care and what is included.
7. **How it works.** Show the placement and retrieval interaction; explain lighting or magnification only if confirmed for that SKU.
8. **Design story.** Why a single-watch dock, why the shape and how the material choices differ.
9. **FAQ / delivery / returns.** Remove purchase anxiety with direct answers.
10. **Close.** A restrained final product image and “Find its place.” CTA.

### Essential product page sections

- Product name, variant, price and finish selection.
- Image gallery with detail views and a scale/context image.
- One-sentence benefit statement.
- Availability, delivery and return information.
- Dimensions and supported watch-size / strap fit information.
- Materials, finish, cleaning and care.
- In-the-box list.
- Confirmed functional specifications.
- Variant comparison and related products.

Do not hide fit and dimensions behind vague marketing copy; compatibility is explicitly an area to resolve in the deck (p. 8).

## 9. Component system

### Navigation

- Wordmark left, short nav in the centre/right, cart at far right.
- Transparent over the hero only if the contrast remains strong; otherwise use an ivory header.
- On scroll, transition to a solid ivory background with a 1 px border—no dramatic blur or heavy shadow.

### Buttons

**Primary:** ink background, ivory text; hover to walnut.  
**Secondary:** transparent background, ink border and ink text; hover to bone.  
**Text link:** simple underline or arrow that shifts by 2–3 px on hover.

Minimum interactive height: 44 px. Always provide visible keyboard focus.

### Product cards

- Large image on an ivory/bone field.
- Product name and variant as the first text line.
- One short differentiator, then price.
- Avoid putting every spec, badge and button on the card.
- Use only genuine labels (e.g. “Wood finish”); avoid arbitrary “Bestseller” badges at launch.

### Variant comparison

Use a clean comparison table on desktop and stacked comparison panels on mobile. Compare only decision-making attributes: finish, material, viewing features, intended use, dimensions and price. Keep undecided specifications marked for confirmation internally—not published as facts.

### Editorial feature block

A generous image and a short text block. Alternate image position between sections; avoid repeating the same four-card grid throughout the page.

### Accordions

Use for dimensions, fit, care, shipping and FAQs. Keep labels concrete (“Which watches fit?”), not generic (“More information”).

### Forms and checkout

Plain, legible labels; concise validation; no decorative form fields. Show all costs and delivery information before payment. Maintain visual continuity with the rest of the site.

## 10. Motion and interaction

Motion should feel precise and physical, like setting an object down—not like a tech demo.

- Hover transitions: 160–220 ms, ease-out.
- Section reveals: 350–500 ms, small movement only (8–16 px) and opacity.
- Product image zoom: subtle 1.02–1.04 scale on hover; never crop important details.
- Variant selection: update image, finish label and price together; indicate selection with more than colour alone.
- Avoid scroll-jacking, forced intro sequences, cursor gimmicks, spinning 3D objects by default and autoplay audio/video.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and keep all content usable without animation.

---

## 11. Accessibility and responsive behaviour

- Meet WCAG AA contrast for body text and interactive controls.
- Use semantic headings in order, meaningful alt text and descriptive link labels.
- Keep body copy at 16 px or above wherever practical.
- Keyboard focus must be clearly visible; controls need accessible names.
- Product gallery and variant selectors must work by keyboard and touch.
- On mobile, show the product and headline immediately; stack copy and actions cleanly.
- Do not make product dimensions, fit or pricing available only through hover.
- Optimise images and avoid loading several large videos on the first screen.

### Responsive behaviour

- **Desktop:** editorial split hero; image can occupy 55–65% of the visual field.
- **Tablet:** retain the split where it still gives the product enough space; otherwise stack.
- **Mobile:** product image first, title next, then concise copy and CTA. Keep the first-screen message focused and avoid carousels that hide key product information.

## 13. Premium experience checklist

Before launch, confirm that the site:

- [ ] Opens with one excellent product image, one message and one obvious next step.
- [ ] Uses real product photography/renders with consistent scale and finish.
- [ ] Shows the object in context as well as isolated on a clean background.
- [ ] Makes Standard / Premium / Grand differences understandable at a glance.
- [ ] Explains fit, dimensions, materials, care, delivery and returns plainly.
- [ ] Uses whitespace, type and image quality—not gold decoration—to signal quality.
- [ ] Avoids repeated shadowed cards and generic ecommerce clutter.
- [ ] Makes no unverified claims about durability, compatibility, magnification or lighting.
- [ ] Works well on mobile, loads quickly and supports keyboard navigation.

## 14. Open questions before the design is final

The pitch deck establishes the direction, but not every production detail. Confirm these before final UI copy and commerce implementation:

1. Final dimensions and the watch case / strap sizes supported by each variant.
2. Whether lighting and magnification will ship in all variants or only selected editions.
3. Final materials, finish names, care requirements and production tolerances.
4. Launch prices, stock status, delivery regions, warranty and returns.
5. Final packaging and whether gifting is a core launch use case.

**North star:** ChronoDock should feel like a small, deliberate design object that earns its place in a room—and makes a watch feel worth displaying even when it is not being worn.
